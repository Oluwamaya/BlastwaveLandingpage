How to Send Bulk SMS in Nigeria Without a Dollar Card or USD Payment

Published September 4, 2026 · 5 min read

If you've tried to send bulk SMS in Nigeria before, you've probably hit the same wall: sign up, paste in your contacts, get ready to launch a campaign — and then discover the only way to pay is a USD-denominated card. For a lot of Nigerian businesses, that's a dead end. Not everyone has a dollar card, and the people who do usually don't want to eat a foreign transaction fee just to text their own customers. Blastwave was built to remove that wall entirely: you can send bulk SMS in Nigeria and pay for every message in naira, from a local card or bank transfer, from the first message you send.

Why Most Bulk SMS Platforms Require a Dollar Card

This isn't an accident — it's a byproduct of where most bulk messaging platforms were built. Global SMS aggregators are usually designed for the US, UK, or European market first, with billing systems that default to USD by design. When a Nigerian business signs up, the pricing, the currency, and the payment gateway all assume you're paying from a dollar-denominated account. Local card networks like Verve often aren't supported at all, and even when Visa or Mastercard technically work, the transaction still gets converted at whatever rate your bank charges that day — on top of the platform's own fees.

The result is a strange situation where a Nigerian business sending SMS to Nigerian customers, about a Nigerian product, still has to route the payment through a foreign currency system that was never built with them in mind.

What to Look for in a Naira-Friendly Bulk SMS Provider

If you're evaluating providers and want to avoid this problem, a few things separate a platform that actually works for Nigerian businesses from one that just tolerates them:

  • Transparent naira pricing. The price you see should be the price you pay — not a USD figure converted at checkout by your card issuer.
  • Local payment rails. Support for Paystack, Verve, and Nigerian bank cards directly, without requiring a workaround.
  • No forced USD top-up minimums. Some platforms require you to load a minimum dollar balance before you can send anything, which locks out smaller businesses.
  • Clear per-message cost. You should be able to see exactly what a campaign will cost before you hit send, in the currency you actually spend.

How Blastwave Solves This for Nigerian Businesses

Blastwave prices every plan in naira and processes payments through Paystack, which means Visa, Mastercard, and Verve all work the same way a local bank transfer would — no dollar card, no manual currency conversion, no surprise fees. Business plans are billed as a flat naira amount for a fixed quota of email and SMS, valid for 30 days. If you're a developer sending SMS through the Blastwave API instead, billing works differently: you top up a naira wallet and each message is deducted as it sends, with free sandbox credit included so you can test your integration before spending anything.

Either way, the currency you see is the currency you pay — and it's naira, from a card you already have.
