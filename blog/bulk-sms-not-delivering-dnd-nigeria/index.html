Why Isn't My Bulk SMS Reaching Some Customers? (The DND Registry Explained)

Published September 6, 2026 · 5 min read

You launch a bulk SMS campaign, the dashboard shows every message as "sent," and yet a chunk of your customers swear they never got it. This is one of the most common — and most confusing — problems Nigerian businesses run into with bulk SMS, and it usually isn't a bug in whatever platform you're using. It's the DND registry doing exactly what it was built to do. Understanding it changes how you plan every campaign you send with Blastwave or any other provider.

What the DND Registry Actually Is

Do Not Disturb (DND) is a registry that Nigerian mobile subscribers can opt into to block promotional and marketing SMS from reaching their line. Once a number is registered, standard promotional SMS routes are blocked from delivering to it — the network filters the message out before it ever reaches the handset. Millions of Nigerian numbers are registered this way, largely because unsolicited SMS spam was a real problem for years before DND existed. If a meaningful share of your contact list is on DND, a percentage of your "sent" messages will quietly disappear, even though nothing technically went wrong on the sending side.

Why Standard Promotional Routes Can't Reach DND Numbers

This is by design, not a flaw. Promotional SMS routes — the ones typically used for marketing blasts, sales announcements, and general campaigns — are exactly the type of traffic DND is meant to filter. If your message reads as marketing content and goes out over a standard promotional route, a DND-registered number simply won't receive it, regardless of which platform sent it or how good your message is.

The Legitimate Way to Reach DND-Registered Numbers

The workaround isn't to trick the system — it's to use the right kind of route for the right kind of message. Transactional and corporate-capable SMS routes, designed for account alerts, confirmations, and other non-marketing communication a customer has a direct relationship with, are generally able to reach DND-registered numbers because they aren't the kind of unsolicited promotional traffic DND was built to block. The distinction matters: a message a customer expects and has a reason to receive (an order update, a payment confirmation, an appointment reminder) is treated differently from a cold marketing blast. Using the correct route for the correct message type, with real consent behind it, is the legitimate way to keep your delivery reliable without running into DND filtering.

How Blastwave Handles This

Blastwave routes messages based on what kind of SMS you're actually sending, so transactional-style messages aren't held to the same filtering as promotional blasts. If you're seeing gaps in delivery on a marketing campaign specifically, that's often a sign the message needs a different route or that a share of your list is DND-registered — both things you can plan around rather than guess at, since Blastwave shows delivery status per recipient in real time so you can see exactly which numbers didn't receive a message and why.
