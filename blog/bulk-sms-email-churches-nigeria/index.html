How Churches Use Bulk SMS and Email for Announcements

Published September 6, 2026 · 5 min read

Announcing something once from the pulpit only reaches the people in the building that day. For churches with a large or growing congregation, bulk SMS and email fills the gap between what gets said on a Sunday and what actually reaches everyone who's part of the church, including the members who missed a particular service.

Common Use Cases

  • Service time reminders. A quick text ahead of a special service, a schedule change, or a midweek program.
  • Special event announcements. Conferences, retreats, and one-off programs reach the whole congregation, not just whoever heard the announcement in person.
  • Prayer requests and updates. Sharing an update on a prayer point or a testimony with everyone who's part of the community.
  • Giving and tithe reminders. Gentle reminders around giving cycles, sent respectfully and only to members who've opted to receive them.

Why a Mix of SMS and Email Works Well

SMS is well suited to short, urgent, same-week reminders — a service time change tomorrow, a last-minute event update — because it's read quickly and doesn't require the recipient to check an inbox. Email works better for longer content: a full weekly bulletin, a detailed event description, or a newsletter recapping recent activities. Using both means urgent things get seen fast, and detailed things get the space they need.

Practical Setup

The starting point is building a congregation contact list with consent — typically collected through a welcome or membership form where people knowingly provide their number or email for church communication. From there, many churches set up recurring weekly reminders (a Sunday service reminder sent Saturday evening, for example) so the message goes out consistently without someone having to remember to send it manually every week.
